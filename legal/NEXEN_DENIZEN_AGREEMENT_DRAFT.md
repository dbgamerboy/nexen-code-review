The duty excludes information Contributor can demonstrate was lawfully known without restriction, becomes public without breach, is received lawfully from an unrestricted source, or is independently developed without use of the confidential information. Legally compelled disclosure is allowed to the required extent, with prior notice and reasonable protective cooperation only where lawful. Ordinary confidential-information duties continue for five years after termination; trade-secret duties continue while the information qualifies for that protection. Personal-data and security obligations continue as applicable law requires.

**Protected-disclosure notice (18 U.S.C. 1833(b)).** An individual is not subject to federal or state trade-secret liability for a disclosure made confidentially to a government official or an attorney solely to report or investigate a suspected legal violation, or in a complaint or other document filed under seal in a proceeding. An individual pursuing a retaliation claim for reporting a suspected violation may disclose the trade secret to their attorney and use it in the proceeding if documents containing it are filed under seal and further disclosure occurs only under court order. This protection covers contractors and consultants where the statute provides. Nothing here bars protected reporting, cooperating with regulators, discussing lawfully protected working conditions, or exercising other nonwaivable rights; prior authorization is not required for protected activity.
